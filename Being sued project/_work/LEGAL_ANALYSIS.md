### 1.7 Trustee's Core Argument

> Under Italian bankruptcy law (art. 41 TUB and art. 110 Legge Fallimentare), a mortgage creditor may continue foreclosure during bankruptcy, but the sum obtained in foreclosure is only **provisional**. Final ranking of all claims takes place in bankruptcy. The creditor is therefore **only entitled to the amount admitted to the passivo**.
>
> Authorities: Cass. 28.09.2018 n. 23482; Cass. 20.04.2022 n. 12673.
>
> Naissance received €629,975.04 from foreclosure.
> Naissance was admitted to passivo for only €572,438.29 (mortgage privilege).
> Therefore Naissance must return the difference: **€57,536.75**.

## PART 2 — STRATEGIC OPTIONS

### 2.1 Approach 1 — Avv. Belardi's "Clerical Error" Strategy

**What he proposes (from his 16.04.2026 email):**
- Argue that Fiorilli's istanza contains a *material error* (errore materiale): the premises cite credit of €1,188,438.29 but the formal request is only €577,199.76
- Ask the Judge to read the documents as a whole and correct the apparent discrepancy via *comune buonsenso*
- Subordinate position: if error is not accepted, seek indemnification from Fiorilli via his professional insurance
- Tertiary: negotiate a reduced settlement with the curator

**Pros:**
- Easiest to plead — the lawyer already supports it
- Documents do support the €1,188,438.29 figure (contract of 28.07.2017)
- If accepted, it entirely defeats the claim

**Cons:**
- **The istanza is NOT internally inconsistent in the way Belardi implies.** §4 explicitly states: "€572,438.29 (1.188.438,29 sottratta la somma di assegnazione pari ad euro 616.000,00)" — i.e. the €572k figure is deliberately net of the €616k auction proceeds. The math is consistent.
- The G.D. already ruled in 2019 on the passivo amount. Revising an admission order six years later on "clerical error" grounds is an uphill fight
- Trustee can reply: the credit chain is irrelevant; what matters is the amount actually admitted, and that ruling is now res judicata in bankruptcy
- Involving Fiorilli via indemnity insurance is a long road, and does not stop the trustee winning against Naissance first

**Overall assessment:** Moderate at best. Addresses a framing issue, not the substantive legal principle the trustee relies on (art. 110 L.F.).

### 2.3 Approach 3 (recommended A) — Attack Finality + Correct the Admitted Amount

**Core thesis:**

The trustee's mathematics depends on two inputs: (i) €629,975.04 received from foreclosure; (ii) €572,438.29 admitted at passivo. **Both inputs can be legitimately challenged.**

**(A) Finality of the 04.04.2019 distribution plan:**

The trustee's pleading rests on the doctrine (Cass. 23482/2018, 12673/2022) that foreclosure allocations are *provisional* and subject to bankruptcy re-ranking. This doctrine is not absolute. The strongest counter-argument is:

- The 04.04.2019 distribution plan was approved by the Enforcement Judge at a hearing and **was not impugned within the terms under art. 512 c.p.c.** (and, under revised bankruptcy doctrine, the right to re-open such orders has time limits and procedural prerequisites that must be pleaded and proved by the trustee)
- If the curator was present at or notified of the 04.04.2019 hearing and did not then raise the now-alleged surplus, the later claim may be estopped or inadmissible
- The trustee must prove on the record exactly what was distributed to whom and when the curator knew — documentary exchange in this proceeding will be critical

**(B) Correct the admitted amount to include the excluded €4,761.47 prededuzione:**

The G.D. rejected the €4,761.47 prededuzione without clear reasoning visible on the face of the passivo ruling. If the passivo ruling is genuinely res judicata (which the trustee's own case logic accepts) then the €4,761.47 was a legitimate legal cost properly claimed. Where the G.D. has erred against Naissance, there is a basis to:

- Argue the trustee cannot selectively rely on res judicata (use €572,438.29 as a fixed ceiling) while ignoring that the ruling itself may have been partly incorrect
- Alternatively, credit Naissance for the €4,761.47 even if not "ipotecario", reducing the claim to **€52,775.28**

**(C) €3,932.00 legal expenses:**

The trustee's own filing says €3,932.00 of Naissance's legal expenses were "not admitted to the bankruptcy liabilities". That €3,932.00 should not be part of any clawback: it was paid as legal expense of the foreclosure, not as an advance on bankruptcy passivo. Reduce by another **€3,932.00** to €48,843.28 on this theory.

**(D) €30,000 procedural advance refund:**

The trustee includes the €30,000 refund of procedural advances in the "€629,975.04 received" calculation. But this €30,000 was Naissance's own money, advanced to fund the procedure, and simply returned. It is not a payment *out of the debtor's estate* at all. Removing this from the claim gives **€18,843.28**.

**Cumulative effect of (A) to (D):** If the finality argument wins, **claim dismissed**. If it fails but the quantum arguments all succeed, **claim reduced by ~€35,000**. Even partial success on (B), (C), (D) materially erodes the claim.

**Pros:**
- Directly engages with the trustee's strongest cited authorities rather than ducking them
- Builds in layered fallbacks (win on finality; if not, win on quantum)
- Rooted in documentary evidence already on the record
- Does not require proving facts the client cannot prove (e.g. the €130k spend)

**Cons:**
- Requires proper legal research of art. 110 L.F. / art. 512 c.p.c. jurisprudence — Belardi must do this work
- Documentary exchange may reveal facts that hurt us (e.g., curator presence at 04.04.2019)
- Does not eliminate all risk — worst outcome is still ~€18k plus interest plus costs

### 2.4 Approach 4 (recommended B) — Negotiated Settlement with Quantum Pressure

**Core thesis:**

Use the Approach 3 arguments as leverage to negotiate a significantly reduced settlement with the curator and close the file. Belardi already mentions that settlement "su basi economiche diverse e minori" is likely possible.

**Target settlement range:**
- Opening position: €15,000–€20,000 all-in (using Approach 3 quantum reductions)
- Realistic landing: €25,000–€35,000 all-in
- Walkaway point: €45,000 (vs claim of €57,536.75 + interest from 01.07.2025 ~€3,000 + costs at hearing → total realistic exposure ~€70,000)

**Pros:**
- Certainty, speed, ends the case before 14.05.2026 hearing
- Saves Naissance further legal fees, court costs and management time
- Trustee has an incentive to settle: recoveries fund creditor distributions, and litigation risk for them is real given the finality argument
- Could be combined with Approach 3: file the defence, then open settlement talks before hearing

**Cons:**
- Paying anything is painful when the client believes they owe nothing
- Does not set a precedent or clear Naissance's reputation
- Risk the curator hardens if he senses weakness (so any settlement overture must come alongside a robust filed defence, not as a substitute)

### 2.5 Overall Recommended Approach

**Do BOTH 3 and 4 in sequence. Specifically:**

1. **File a substantive defence by 04.05.2026** built on Approach 3 (finality + quantum arguments), drafted to make the trustee's counsel uncomfortable about winning at hearing. Required components:
   - Detailed legal briefing on Cass. 23482/2018 and 12673/2022 — what exactly those cases decided, how the facts differ here, any later or distinguishing case law
   - Production of the 04.04.2019 distribution plan and hearing minutes (trustee will produce as doc 3; cross-check for curator presence/knowledge)
   - Direct challenge to the €30,000 "refund" inclusion — this is an obvious own-goal by the trustee
   - Secondary challenge to €3,932.00 "not admitted legal expenses" inclusion
   - Reservation of right to claim €4,761.47 prededuzione credit
   - Reservation of right to bring third-party action against Avv. Fiorilli for professional negligence (if the admission decision is found wrongly limited)

2. **Open settlement dialogue** with the curator immediately after filing, proposing a negotiated resolution in the €15,000–€30,000 range, on strictly "without prejudice" terms.

3. **DO NOT rely primarily on the "clerical error" theory.** It is weaker than Belardi thinks because the istanza's math is internally consistent. Mention it only as tertiary support, not headline argument.

4. **DO NOT lead with "€1.188m credit proves we're owed more".** The trustee already concedes the credit. Use it only to explain why Naissance would never have deliberately under-claimed at passivo — to support context, not as the operative legal argument.

5. **Involve Avv. Fiorilli via professional indemnity AFTER** the Naissance defence is filed and the picture is clearer. Do not let the "sue Fiorilli" angle distract from winning the principal case first.

6. **Close the €30,000-refund gap URGENTLY.** If Grahame genuinely paid €30,000 in procedural advances that were refunded to him, this creates a misleading picture of having paid costs of €130,000 when the net-net is lower. We must be internally clear on actual net expenditure before arguing set-off.
