## The claim in one paragraph

The Fallimento of Naissance Srl, through trustee Avv. Stefania Cericola, is asking the Tribunale di Roma to order Grahame Malenga to pay approximately **€85,000** into the bankruptcy estate. The trustee's theory: Grahame, as the creditor who funded the *concordato preventivo* in 2014, was paid €230,000 out of the final distribution in 2024; the estate subsequently recovered surplus funds (principally a tax refund); under Italian case law the surplus must be returned to the company/estate even where a distribution plan has already been executed, and Grahame's original credit was exhausted by the 2024 payment so he has no entitlement to hold the further €85k.

## What the initial analysis concluded

Read at face value, the defence file is attractive. The 2018 Istanza by Avv. Fiorilli sets out a clear narrative: Grahame wired **€1.188 million** into the procedure between 2010 and 2014 to rescue Naissance, of which only a fraction was ever formally "recognised" as an ammissible credit by the procedure. Grahame's own position letter records cost outflows of roughly €130,000 and argues those must be netted. The distribution plan of 2024 was approved by the Giudice Delegato and not appealed; it appeared to be final. On first reading the defence looked to have a **50-60% chance of full success** and the downside looked manageable.

## What changed under challenge

Five material weaknesses emerged:

**1. The "finality of the distribution plan" argument does not answer the trustee's case.**
The trustee is not re-opening the 2024 plan. She is applying the separate rule that *surplus* recovered by the estate *after* distribution must flow back through the estate, and that a creditor whose credit is already satisfied has no right to retain more. The leading authorities (Cass. Civ. 23482/2018 and Cass. Civ. 12673/2022, the line typically cited in this fact-pattern) treat the restitution duty as arising *ex lege* — by operation of law — and therefore *not* cut off by the finality of the prior plan. The initial defence instinct ("the plan is closed, end of story") is weaker than it looked.

**2. The €1.188m figure is a gross inflow figure, not a recognised credit.**
Fiorilli's 2018 Istanza argued for recognition of a larger credit; it did not secure it. What Grahame has in the procedure is the credit actually *ammesso allo stato passivo* (admitted to the creditor schedule), not the €1.188m gross wire total. Arguing "I am owed €1.188m so the €85k is mine" is likely to be rejected quickly and costs credibility on the points that do matter.

**3. The €130k "cost" claim is only about €77k documented.**
The FX/receipts file supports roughly €77,000 of out-of-pocket costs with contemporaneous evidence. About €30,000 of the asserted total appears in the trustee's own filing as a payment *to* Grahame — i.e. it is a refund already received, not a cost still owed. Asserting the full €130k risks being caught double-counting and is the kind of error that invites the judge to distrust the rest of the file.

**4. Collaterally attacking the prededuzione exclusion is a dead end.**
The 2018 Istanza's attempt to have Grahame's claim treated in *prededuzione* was not successful at the time. Re-litigating that in the current defence would be a collateral attack on a long-settled procedural question; Italian insolvency courts dislike this, and it would draw fire away from the arguments that could actually win.

**5. The numbers and the timeline favour settlement, not trial.**
Realistic range of outcomes, once the above is priced in: roughly **15-20% chance of full win**, **20-25% chance of partial reduction** (trustee accepts c. €30-50k net), **55-65% chance of full loss** at trial with costs. Full-loss exposure is in the region of €85-95k once legal costs and *spese di giustizia* are added. A negotiated settlement in the €40-45k range materially improves the expected outcome and removes timeline risk.


## The revised recommended strategy

**Primary track: file a tight, credible defence by 04 May 2026, then settle.**

Avv. Belardi should be instructed this week, in writing, with a clear drafting timetable that leaves at least three working days before 04 May for Grahame to review a draft. The defence (*Comparsa di Costituzione e Risposta*) should be built around two arguments — no more — so that it reads like a focused, credible filing rather than a kitchen-sink defence:

  **Argument A — Netting of documented out-of-pocket costs.** Grahame advanced the procedure funds and incurred roughly €77,000 of documented external costs (FX fees, legal/professional fees paid to support the procedure, wire charges) for which there is contemporaneous evidence in the payments file. These should be netted against any restitution obligation. This is the strongest defensive point and is supportable from the paper trail.

  **Argument B — Quantum challenge to the trustee's €85k figure.** The trustee's own schedule appears to double-count at least one item (the c. €30,000 shown as a payment *to* Grahame that is in substance a refund already paid, not a cost outstanding). The €85k headline number should be reconstructed from first principles and challenged line-by-line; a realistic net exposure is materially lower than €85k on the trustee's own numbers.

**What the defence should *not* do:**
  - Do not argue the distribution plan's finality bars the claim.
  - Do not assert the €1.188m as a recognised credit.
  - Do not attack the €30k refund as improper or suspicious — it is documented and the attack will rebound.
  - Do not collaterally re-open the prededuzione exclusion.
  - Do not claim the full €130k of costs; stop at what the receipts support.

**Parallel track: open settlement dialogue now.** The trustee's economic interest is recovery into the estate, not litigation for its own sake. A *proposta transattiva* in the €40-45k range, offered before the 04 May filing or immediately after, has a realistic prospect of acceptance because it gives the estate cash certainty and avoids the trustee's own cost exposure at trial. Settlement should be explored in parallel with preparing the defence, not instead of it — the defence is the BATNA that makes the settlement offer credible.

## Confidence level in the revised position

The revised position should be read with the following levels of confidence:

- **High confidence** that the initial "plan is final, case closed" instinct is wrong under current Cassazione jurisprudence on post-distribution surplus.
- **High confidence** that asserting the full €130k of costs and the €1.188m gross credit would damage the defence.
- **Moderate-to-high confidence** that a focused two-argument defence plus parallel settlement is the rational strategy on the facts available.
- **Moderate confidence** on the probability ranges. The 15-20% full-win / 55-65% full-loss range is a reasonable central estimate for a file of this shape, but it is sensitive to two things that only Belardi can verify: (a) the exact amount admitted to the stato passivo in Grahame's name, and (b) whether the court file contains anything — a prior decreto, a stipulation, a communication — that changes the picture. Either could move the probabilities materially in either direction.
- **Lower confidence** on the settlement range. €40-45k is an informed estimate of where a rational trustee might land, but it is not a negotiated number. The actual landing zone will depend on the trustee's view of her own litigation risk and on what Belardi reads in her posture.
