## What changes the analysis

### 1. The 2018 Istanza is internally inconsistent — the "errore materiale" argument is stronger than the original analysis said

The full text of Avv. Fiorilli's 14.12.2018 Istanza (`email 7
attachment.pdf`) reveals:

- **Clause 3** says Naissance asserts a credit of **€.1.118.438,29**
  against Agricola Gavioli.
- **Clause 4** parenthetical says **€572.438,29 (1.188.438,29
  sottratta la somma di assegnazione pari ad euro 616.000,00)**.

The arithmetic only works for €1,188,438.29 (1,188,438.29 − 616,000 =
572,438.29). €1,118,438.29 − €616,000 = €502,438.29, which does not
reconcile to anything. So clause 3 is almost certainly a typo for
€1,188,438.29.

This is a **genuine internal inconsistency** in the document the
trustee is relying on. The original `LEGAL_ANALYSIS.md` said the
Istanza's math is internally consistent and therefore Belardi's
"clerical error" strategy is weak (scored 5/10). **That was wrong.**
Clause 3 and clause 4 contradict each other; the judge can reasonably
be asked to treat clause 3 as the true figure, clause 4 as the
typo-afflicted one, or vice versa, because the documents attached to
the Istanza (the 28.07.2017 credit assignment contracts) are all
consistent with €1,188,438.29.

Belardi's own 17.04.2026 email confirms he sees "€.1.118.438,29" in
his copy — suggesting either (a) the typo is genuinely in the filed
document, or (b) his copy has the same typo. Either way, the argument
is live.

**Revised score for Belardi's "errore materiale" strategy: 6–7/10**,
not 5/10. Still not a standalone winner (the trustee's fall-back
argument is that the G.D.'s 2019 admission order itself is res
judicata for €572,438.29, regardless of what Fiorilli wrote), but it
is a stronger platform than first assessed.

### 2. Evidenced cost outflows move from €77k to ~€152k

The earlier FX bundle summary evidenced only two wires (€57,000 on
26.04.2018 and €20,000 on 01.05.2018 = €77,000). The late additions
add one more wire and two cheques, but — **important correction per
Grahame's confirmation (WhatsApp 18.04.2026 19:29)** — the €30,000
FX wire of 05.06.2018 is **the same payment** as one of the two
cheques. The wire is the FX execution of that cheque, not a separate
outflow. So the real picture is:

| Outflow | Evidence | €  |
|---|---|---|
| Wire 26.04.2018 to Argo Ge.Re.Cre. | FX confirmation (Thomas Exchange) — corresponds to the €57,110.15 Gavioli cost schedule in email 6 | 57,000.00 |
| Wire 01.05.2018 to Argo Ge.Re.Cre. | FX confirmation | 20,000.00 |
| €30,000 cheque / wire 05.06.2018 *(same payment)* | Cheque image (email 2) + FX confirmation (email 3) | 30,000.00 |
| €45,000 cheque | Cheque image (email 2) | 45,000.00 |
| **Total evidenced** | | **~152,000** |

This is still consistent with Grahame's "€130,000+ costs" position
in his 18.04.2026 email to Belardi, and decisively defeats the prior
analysis's evidentiary gap — but it is **not** cumulative with the
€30k wire, which had been mistakenly counted twice in an earlier
draft. The €30,000 refund point in §3 below is therefore clearer
and cleaner: one €30,000 went out from Naissance in 2018 and the
same €30,000 came back via the 2019 distribution. That is the
double-count.

### 3. The €30,000 "refund" in the trustee's claim is now much more concrete

The trustee's Ricorso includes a €30,000 figure described as a
"refund of procedural expense advance previously paid by Naissance"
in its calculation of what Naissance received via the enforcement
route. The late additions show:

- Naissance issued a **€30,000 cheque** to the procedure, executed
  as an FX wire to Argo Ge.Re.Cre. on **05.06.2018** (the cheque
  and wire are the same payment — confirmed by Grahame).
- The **04.04.2019 distribution plan then refunded €30,000 to
  Naissance.**

That is almost certainly the same €30,000. This strengthens the
quantum-attack argument materially: the €30,000 in the trustee's
"€629,975.04 received" figure is not a payment from the estate to
Naissance in any economically meaningful sense — it is a return to
Naissance of its own advance. Including it in the restitution claim
is **double-counting**.

If the €30,000 is removed from the trustee's arithmetic, the claim
drops from €57,536.75 to **€27,536.75**. This is the single biggest
point of leverage for Belardi.

### 4. Market-value evidence for the Chianciano property is now solid

Three contemporaneous data points, all from before the 28.02.2018
auction:

- **06.09.2017**: Marco Gavioli expresses interest in buying at
  €550,000 (email 5).
- **12.09.2017**: Marco Gavioli raises the offer to €620,000 before
  the next auction (email 4).
- **"Accettazione di una transazione a €630.000 prima dell'asta"**
  referenced by Grahame in the 18.04.2026 email (email 7 text).
- **Auction clearing price**: €616,000.

This supports a narrative that the €616,000 auction price is a fair
market clearing price, not a windfall. It undermines any trustee
argument that Naissance got the property cheap at auction and is
now holding hidden value above the admitted claim. This helps the
finality argument because it reinforces that the 04.04.2019
distribution plan reflected a fair-value realisation.
